What am I looking at?

This clock tells time the way the ancient Babylonians did: in seasonal hours. Daylight is split into 12 equal hours and night into 12 equal hours — so an "hour" isn't fixed at 60 minutes. It stretches in summer and shrinks in winter, and day-hours differ from night-hours. The gold arc above the horizon is daytime; the indigo arc below is night. Watch how their sizes change with the season and your latitude.

The readout is Hh UU:GG plus a day-part:

  • H — the seasonal hour (simanu), 0–11 since sunrise or sunset.
  • uš — ~4 minutes (1° of the sun's motion); counts up to a location-dependent max per hour.
  • gar — ~4 seconds; 60 to an uš.

The four day-parts, Babylonian AM/PM:

  • ASR after sunrise · BST before sunset
  • AST after sunset · BSR before sunrise

What's different from the original

Sunrise and sunset are computed in your browser from the sunrise equation — no external APIs, works offline. Location comes from your device (with your permission) or the picker above.
